What Westside does well

  1. 01
    It owns its platforms.

    Wesness (a 25,000-strong women's run community in four cities), YNG (4,000 creators, 6M views in Q4 FY26) and FRONT ROW (150 culture-shapers, 80 looks) are Westside's own. They are not rented celebrity deals.

  2. 02
    Some IPs already start at a store.

    Wesness runs set off from Westside Gachibowli, New Commercial Street and D.N. Nagar. The instinct to use the store as a venue is already there.

  3. 03
    It runs on CRM.

    With 11.85M active WestStyleClub members behind over 90% of revenue, Westside can tie a visit to a bill in a way few Indian retailers can.

  4. 04
    It is getting fast.

    An AI design suite took weekly output from about 50 designs to 400–500, with 30-day lead times targeted on trend pieces. That is close to Zara's product speed.

Where the journey breaks

  1. 01
    Content rarely names a store.

    In the public record we found no store-tagged or city-tagged series and no "come and try it" calls to action. Shoppers' own "Westside new arrivals" haul videos on YouTube fill that gap.

  2. 02
    Results stop at views.

    YNG reports views, engagements and shares. Wesness reports registrations. Nothing public connects either to store entries or bills.

  3. 03
    The IPs live in four cities. The growth is elsewhere.

    Expansion is aimed at Delhi NCR, Bengaluru, Hyderabad and the north-east. Wesness has no presence in the north or north-east yet.

  4. 04
    New stores open quietly.

    About 40 stores opened between October 2025 and March 2026 with no visible launch playbook. Each one is a first impression in a new neighbourhood.

  5. 05
    No hero moment for the biggest seasons.

    We found no Westside film or campaign for Diwali 2025 or the 2025–26 wedding season. The festive story was carried by product pages and blog guides.

Public-source scan, 15 Oct 2025–15 Oct 2026. Absence in public sources is not proof that something didn't happen, and we have no view of Westside's internal measurement. Trent reports like-for-like growth for its fashion portfolio, not for Westside alone. Full list under Sources.

The Zara lesson

Zara has 22 stores in India. Westside has 311. Win where Zara isn't.

Zara barely advertises. Its stores do the work: prime windows, constant newness, scarcity and one consistent editorial eye. In India that model is stalling. FY26 revenue slipped 1% to ₹2,749 crore and profit fell 32%, across 22 stores in 13 cities. Westside can bring the same discipline to a neighbourhood near every customer.

On our estimate, Westside already sells more than Zara India in total: roughly ₹5,000 crore against ₹2,749 crore. Zara's lead is per store, where it sells several times more. Footfall per store is how Westside closes that gap.

Trent holds 20% of Zara India (Inditex about 80%) as a financial investment, so we frame this as learning from Zara, not attacking it. Trent does not publish Westside revenue; our estimate is derived from about ₹300 crore of online sales at about 6% of the total.

04 · The idea

Seen at Westside

Every post ends at a store. Every store becomes the next post.

We turn Westside's stores into the media. Local stylists make the looks, the trial room becomes the studio, the window becomes a weekly drop, and every piece of content carries a store's name. Then we count who walks in.

  1. 01Every piece of content names a store.
  2. 02Every pilot store has a reason to visit this week.
  3. 03Every visit can be booked on WhatsApp.
  4. 04Every bill can be tagged to the drop through WestStyleClub.
  5. 05Every result is reported per store.
Content IP · weekly

The Trial Room

01 · The Trial Room→ Saves · visits

A short-form series shot inside Westside trial rooms. One piece styled three ways on real people by a local stylist, ending on the store's address.

Stylist network

The Style Collective

02 · The Style Collective→ Bookings

30–50 local stylists for the pilot city, paid per look and credited on the tag. It grows to 300 in a city and 1,500 nationally, but only if the pilot clears its threshold. YNG creators and FRONT ROW's 30 New Faces join first.

Weekly drop · Fridays

The Window

03 · The Window→ Fri–Sun entries

Every Friday a Collective stylist restyles the hero mannequin, and it is revealed on social first. A QR code on the mannequin shows three ways to wear it and books a try-on.

In-store experience

Style Call

04 · Style Call→ Show-ups · bills

Free 20-minute styling slots on weekend evenings, when footfall peaks, booked on WhatsApp. Store teams are trained as Style Associates for the first two minutes of every visit. A "pick your festive look" vote runs at the entrance.

Store-launch format

First Look

05 · First Look→ Day 1–30 footfall

The 21 days before a new Westside opens: neighbourhood creators, a "People of [city]" film, hoardings that give the distance to the store, founding-member WestStyleClub invites and opening-weekend Style Calls.

Distribution

The 72-hour Burst

06 · The 72-hour Burst→ Catchment reach

Every drop closes with a burst: 150–200 vetted clipper pages, Collective stylists, YNG creators, city PR and geo-fenced paid media within about 5 km. Google Business Profile posts and WhatsApp messages go to nearby members.

Built on what Westside already owns: Wesness runs end at the store with a Style Call; FRONT ROW's Tuxedo Street dance-offs and 30 New Faces casting are hosted on shop floors; YNG creators join the Collective. The Collective sizes (30–50, 300, 1,500) are proposed programme sizes, not existing Westside programmes.

Quantities are indicative and are fixed in the scope we return after the working session.

Measurement & care

The primary metric is store entries per trading day from the people counter. Supporting metrics are bills ÷ entries, average bill value, WestStyleClub-tagged bills, Style Call bookings and show-ups, and QR scans. We use only aggregated store-level data; member-level matching happens only with consent under India's DPDP Act. Details in the Scorecard.

Returns

If the numbers don't move, you lose nothing. In contract terms, if verified footfall at the pilot store does not beat the matched stores by the threshold we agree up front, Westside pays no agency fee. Westside approves production and media spend upfront, at cost.

We also agree in writing what happens if the read is inconclusive, for example after a stock-out or a mall closure: we either extend the window or call it.

If you prefer a different structure, a reduced base fee plus a bonus at agreed lift levels works too. So does a contingent pilot paired with first rights to the roll-out.

What we need from Westside
  • One store with confirmed stock for the drop
  • Daily people-counter data for the pilot and comparator stores: 12 weeks back, the same weeks last year, and the live weeks
  • A two-hour store-team session and a store manager who owns the pilot
  • Brand approvals within 48 hours during the live weeks
  • WhatsApp and SMS sends to opted-in WestStyleClub members near the store

08 · Scorecard

Footfall first. Bills in context.

We will claim only what the door counter can see. The method is a standard difference-in-differences read: the pilot store's change, minus the change at matched Westside stores over the same weeks.

(Pilot live − pilot baseline) − (comparators live − comparators baseline) = incremental entries per trading day

Agreed before launch

Threshold, comparator stores, measurement windows and exclusions (stock-outs, store closures, mall events) are signed off before day one. No moving the goalposts, either way.

Honest about power

One store over a few weeks can reliably detect a lift of roughly 10% or more. If you want to see smaller effects, we add test stores rather than stretch the claim.

Private by design

Aggregated counter data does the main read. WestStyleClub matching for platform lift studies happens only with consent, under the DPDP Act 2023 and the 2025 Rules.

Why we expect it to move: in a randomised field experiment with a US apparel retailer, 84% of the sales lift from online ads landed in physical stores (Lewis & Reiley). Closer to home, Google reports that 26% of Tanishq's store sales had a Google touchpoint (a platform-reported figure). Brands rarely prove this because they don't measure at store level. The pilot is that measurement.
